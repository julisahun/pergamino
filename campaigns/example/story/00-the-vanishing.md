# The Vanishing

Curdmoor's Grand Wheel has sat under its ceremonial dome in Cheese Square for
eleven generations, ever since a travelling god named Cheddrick — evicted
from his last temple over unpaid rent, and unwilling to go through that again
— blessed a passing dairy cart instead and settled for being worshipped in
wheel form. Nobody has ever properly explained why the town went along with
this. It has simply always been true, the way Tuesdays are true.

The night before the Cheese Festival, the dome is found standing open and the
Wheel is gone. No forced locks, no ransom note, no sign of a struggle — just
a stone pedestal with a wheel-shaped dent in it, some rind crumbs, and one
claw mark far too large for anything that should have fit through the door.

Mayor Bartleby Crumbwell, up for re-election the week after the festival,
hires the party in Cheese Square before the brass band has even finished
tuning up. His terms are simple: find the Wheel before dawn, or explain to
eleven generations of tradition — and several thousand furious voters — why
it isn't there.
